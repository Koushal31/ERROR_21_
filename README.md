Hackathon Success Guide for Today
Since you're attending a hackathon today, here's a comprehensive guide to help you maximize your chances of success and have a great experience!

Essential Pre-Event Preparation
Team and Idea Setup

If you haven't already, quickly review the hackathon theme and judging criteria to align your project accordingly​

Form or finalize your team with complementary skills: ideally one coder, one designer/UI-UX person, one strategic thinker, and one strong presenter​

Arrive with at least a rough idea rather than wasting precious hours during the event on ideation​

Technical Setup

Ensure your development environment is ready with all necessary software pre-installed​

Download any IDEs, frameworks, or tools you might need since internet can be slow during the event​

Have your GitHub account set up and ready for version control

Prepare boilerplate code or templates for common components like login forms, navigation, and basic layouts​

What to Bring Today
Essential Items

Laptop and charger (most critical items)​

Phone and charger

External monitor if possible (helps with presentations and productivity)​

Surge protector/power strip to share outlets​

Portable battery pack for devices​

Comfort and Health

Jacket or layers (venue temperatures vary widely)​

Reusable water bottle to stay hydrated​

Noise-canceling headphones or earplugs for focus and sleep​

Deodorant and basic hygiene items​

Any personal medications​

Professional Items

Updated resume for sponsor networking​

Business cards if you have them

Pen, paper, and sticky notes for brainstorming​

Winning Strategy During the Event
Focus on Simplicity and Execution

Choose one clear, real-world problem to solve rather than trying to build an all-in-one product​

Prioritize a working MVP (Minimum Viable Product) over complex features​

Stick with technologies you know well rather than experimenting with new tools​

Time Management

Break your time into phases: ideation (first few hours), development, testing/debugging, and final presentation preparation​

Leave at least 2-3 hours before deadline for presentation preparation and submission​

Take strategic breaks and power naps if it's a long event​

Team Collaboration

Assign clear roles early and stick to them​

Use project management tools like Trello or Notion to track progress​

Have regular check-ins to assess progress and make quick decisions​

Creating an Impressive Demo
Presentation Structure
Follow this proven format for your final pitch:

Problem Statement - Clearly explain what problem you're solving​

Solution Demo - Show your working prototype in action​

Technical Implementation - Briefly highlight key technologies and challenges overcome​

Team Strengths - Introduce team members and their relevant skills​

Future Potential - Explain the long-term impact and next steps​

Demo Best Practices

Make it interactive - let judges use your product themselves​

Prepare 2-3 solid test cases that showcase your solution effectively​

Keep it visual and engaging with props or multiple screens if possible​

Have backup plans ready in case of technical difficulties (screenshots, recorded demos)​

Practice your pitch multiple times and stick to the time limit​

Technical Backup Strategies
Prepare for Common Issues

Save important code locally and push to GitHub frequently

Have offline documentation and resources downloaded

Prepare screen recordings of your working demo in case live demo fails​

Keep your most confident team member ready to handle the presentation​

Documentation

Create clear documentation of your project including problem statement, solution, tech stack, and team roles​

Maintain a well-written README file in your code repository

Include live demo links or video walkthroughs if possible​

Networking and Learning Opportunities
Make Connections

Engage with mentors and ask for feedback throughout the event​

Network with other participants and sponsors during breaks​

Connect with people on LinkedIn after meaningful conversations

Don't just focus on winning - prioritize learning and building relationships​

Final Success Tips
Mindset and Approach

Stay calm and adapt quickly when things go wrong​

Focus on collaboration and clear communication with your team​

Remember that judges are human - tell a compelling story about your solution​

Even if you don't win, you'll gain valuable experience, connections, and a portfolio project​

Avoid Common Pitfalls

Don't get distracted by extra features or nice-to-haves​

Don't spend time on sponsor events if you're focused on winning​

Don't ignore your presentation until the last minute​

Don't try to learn new technologies during the event​

Remember, hackathons are as much about the journey as the destination. Focus on building something functional, tell a great story about why it matters, and enjoy the collaborative energy. Good luck with your hackathon today!

🔗 API Endpoints
Authentication 🔑
POST /api/signup — Create account

POST /api/login — Login

POST /api/logout — Logout

GET /api/user/current — Current user

GET /api/user/profile — Profile & stats

Campaigns 🚩
GET /api/projects — List campaigns (?category=&search=)

GET /api/projects/:id — Campaign details

POST /api/projects — Create campaign (auth required)

POST /api/projects/:id/donate — Donate (auth required)

POST /api/projects/:id/update — Post update (auth required)
